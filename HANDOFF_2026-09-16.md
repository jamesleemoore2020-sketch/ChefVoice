## 3. DECISIONS
- Title extraction requires a mandatory pronoun ("I'm"/"we're"/"I am"/"we are") before the making/cooking verbs — a bare imperative like "make four burger patties" is a real, common mid-recipe instruction and would otherwise be mistaken for a title just as easily as "we're making hamburgers" is.
- Title is matched per-sentence "unit" (each raw ASR segment, split only on hard punctuation `.!?`), not through the existing `splitNarration()` clause-splitter and not against the whole flattened transcript. `splitNarration()`'s verb-boundary logic (needed elsewhere to isolate method instructions) can separate a leading pronoun from its verb across two clauses, silently defeating a pattern that needs both in the same piece of text. A live ASR segment is itself a natural, pause-delimited sentence boundary, which is a more reliable stop point than reconstructing one from a blindly rejoined string.
- The "making/make/cooking/cook" title form is further gated to the first two units (chefs say the name at the very start); the "this is my recipe for X" / "this recipe is X" forms are allowed in any unit since they're distinctive framing sentences a chef wouldn't say mid-step.
- A direct "prep time N minutes, cook time N minutes" statement is pulled out of the transcript before any other parsing runs and overrides the inferred verb-duration estimate when both are present — a direct statement is higher-confidence than an inference, and leaving it in place either silently drops it (no "prep" action-word match) or produces a meaningless "Cook time." step (bare "cook" is a recognized action word).
- Bare cut-state modifiers (ground/diced/sliced/chopped/minced/grated/shredded/crushed/boneless/skinless/peeled/cubed) are rejected as standalone ingredient names via an explicit new word list, not a general heuristic — matches this parser's existing narrow, evidence-based convention rather than guessing.
- Title/prep/cook time were deliberately NOT added to `shared/golden-cooking-corpus.tsv` — that TSV is the cross-platform ingredient/step contract specifically; this is new scope on both platforms, so it's tested as ordinary `@Test`/`test()` functions in each platform's own test file instead (mirrored 1:1 between the two).

## 4. RULED OUT
- Whole-transcript-string title matching (one regex against the full joined transcript): over-captured into the next sentence whenever ASR segments have no punctuation between them — confirmed against the real nachos golden fixture, where "make some nachos" ran on into "one pack should feed at least two people" with no way to know where to stop. Replaced by per-segment-unit matching, where the unit's own natural end is the stop point.
- Gating title matching to "the first 2 clauses produced by `splitNarration()`": that splitter's own verb-boundary insertion fragments a phrase like "Today I'm going to make chili" into "Today I'm going to" | "make chili", stranding the pronoun in a different clause than the verb — so a pattern requiring both together never matched at all. Abandoned for the per-segment-unit approach (point 2 above), which sidesteps `splitNarration()` for this purpose entirely.
- An optional (non-mandatory) pronoun in the title-making pattern: caused two real regressions against existing golden-corpus fixtures — "make four burger patties" and "cook 20 minutes" were both extracted as if they were the title, and the matching step then got deleted as a "redundant title announcement." Fixed by making the pronoun mandatory (see Decisions).
- Merging the "Salt or pepper" + "Salt" duplicate that can appear when a segment break falls between "salt" and "or pepper": a prefix-based merge (treating one ingredient name as a truncated form of another) risks conflating genuinely different ingredients — e.g. "onion" vs "onion powder", or "chili" vs "chili powder" in the exact recipe that surfaced this. Left as a known, documented gap; the existing manual delete (×) button handles it.
